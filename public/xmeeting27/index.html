The largest bioinformatics conference in Brazil

For more than twenty years, X-Meeting has brought together the Brazilian and international bioinformatics community. Across its editions it has welcomed tens of thousands of researchers, students, and industry participants. In 2027 we expect 800 to 1,000 attendees in Niterói, on Guanabara Bay, for four days of research, methods, and conversation across biology and computation.

Voices from past editions

A selection of the distinguished scientists who have spoken at previous editions of X-Meeting.

Nobel Prize in Chemistry, 2002

Kurt Wüthrich

ETH Zürich / Scripps Research · Switzerland / USA

Awarded the Nobel Prize for developing nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy to determine the three-dimensional structure of biological macromolecules in solution.

Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine, 1993

Richard J. Roberts

New England Biolabs · USA / United Kingdom

Awarded the Nobel Prize for the discovery of split genes (introns) and the mechanism of eukaryotic mRNA splicing.

Father of the Human Genome Project

Charles DeLisi

Boston University · USA

Widely recognized as the father of the Human Genome Project, which he conceived, funded, and initiated during his tenure at the U.S. Department of Energy.

Co-author of the Smith–Waterman algorithm

Michael Waterman

University of Southern California · USA

A founding father of computational biology who co-developed the Smith–Waterman local sequence alignment algorithm and the Lander–Waterman model for genome mapping.

Pioneer of protein crystallography

Sir Tom Blundell

University of Cambridge · United Kingdom

A pioneer in protein X-ray crystallography and structural bioinformatics who co-developed structural homology modeling methods and founded Astex Pharmaceuticals.

Structural biology of RNA

Elena Conti

Max Planck Institute of Biochemistry / EMBL · Germany

A world-leading structural biologist renowned for elucidating the atomic mechanisms of eukaryotic RNA transport, quality control, and exosome-mediated degradation.

Non-coding RNA and the end of “junk DNA”

John Mattick

UNSW Sydney / Garvan Institute of Medical Research · Australia

Revolutionized modern genomics by overturning the “junk DNA” dogma and showing that long non-coding RNAs form the regulatory architecture of complex organisms.

Reactome, IntAct, PRIDE, and BioModels

Henning Hermjakob

EMBL-EBI · United Kingdom

Head of Molecular Systems at EMBL-EBI, leading global open-science bioinformatics infrastructures including Reactome, IntAct, PRIDE, and BioModels.

Keynote speaker, X-Meeting 2026

Alessandra Carbone

Sorbonne Université / Institut Universitaire de France · France

Winner of the Joliot-Curie Prize, renowned for mathematical and machine learning models that predict protein–protein interactions, coevolving residues, and viral evolution.

Cantareira Innovation District

Distrito de Inovação da Cantareira

Niterói, Rio de Janeiro, Brazil

The district occupies the historic Cantareira station building on the shore of Guanabara Bay, for more than a century a departure point for crossing the bay. Today it connects universities, research centers, startups, companies, and public institutions around research, technology, and innovation.

Niterói lies across Guanabara Bay from the city of Rio de Janeiro.
